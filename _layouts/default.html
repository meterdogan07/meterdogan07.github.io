Mete Erdogan

I am a first-year PhD student in Electrical Engineering at Stanford University, working with Mert Pilanci and James Zou. My research focuses on efficient ML systems and agentic AI.

I am particularly interested in how language models can reason, adapt, use tools, and interact in complex environments. My work spans agentic AI, efficient sequence modeling, model compression, and methods for making large-scale learning systems more computationally and memory efficient. More broadly, I draw on ideas from optimization, information theory, and signal processing to develop scalable and adaptive learning methods.

Before Stanford, I worked at EPFL with Volkan Cevher on language model pruning, compression, spectral interpretability (e.g., PCA/CCA), and non-Euclidean optimization methods for deep learning. I completed dual B.S. degrees in Electrical and Electronics Engineering and Computer Science at Koç University, where I worked with Alper T. Erdogan on deep learning theory and biologically plausible learning rules alternative to backpropagation.

Research

I am broadly interested in building capable, efficient, and adaptive AI systems. Current research directions include:

(i) Agentic AI — studying how language models can reason, use tools, interact, and coordinate with other agents;

(ii) Efficient sequence modeling — developing scalable attention, memory, and alternative sequence-modeling mechanisms for long-context reasoning and inference;

(iii) Efficient learning and model adaptation — improving the computational and memory efficiency of training, adapting, and deploying large language and multimodal models.
